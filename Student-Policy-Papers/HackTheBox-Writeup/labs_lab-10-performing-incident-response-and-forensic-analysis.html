Lab 10: Performing Incident Response and Forensic Analysis

Challenges: There was a lot of information to go through when analyzing PCAP files and disk images. Trying to piece together what happened and when felt like solving a puzzle. Writing the incident report was also challenging because I needed to include all relevant details while staying clear and organized. It was tough balancing technical evidence with easy to understand explanations. I had to be extra careful not to miss anything important. Process & Tools: I used Wireshark to review PCAP files and searched disk images for keylogger evidence. I tracked timestamps and email messages that tied into the attack timeline. I documented everything in a structured incident response report, including who was involved, how the attack happened, and what systems were affected. I also looked for signs of spyware and documented red flags that pointed to phishing. Each step built up the full picture of the incident. Takeaways: This lab showed me what it takes to conduct a real forensic investigation. I learned how to trace events through log files, analyze evidence, and build a timeline. I also saw how important good documentation is when reporting an incident. Overall, it gave me a deeper appreciation for how security teams respond to and learn from breaches.
